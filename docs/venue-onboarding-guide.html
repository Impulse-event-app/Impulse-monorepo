Chapter one

Before you start.

Gather these first. The verification step asks for all of it, and it is far quicker to collect it once than to stop halfway.

To sign in

  • The email address and password we sent you. There is no self-signup — we create the account for you. If you lose the password you can reset it yourself from the sign-in screen.

About the venue

  • A good photo of the room. Landscape works best. JPG or PNG, under 5 MB.
  • Your street address including the street number, and the suburb.
  • Your opening hours for each day of the week.

About the business

  • Your registered company name, exactly as it appears on your ASIC record.
  • Your ABN — eleven digits.
  • Bank account details for your payouts: account name, BSB and account number. The account has to be in the business's name.
  • The full name, email and date of birth of every director, plus anyone holding 25% or more of the shares.

Documents to have scanned

  • Photo ID for each of those people — in colour, unexpired. A driver's licence needs the front and the back saved as two separate files.
  • Stripe may ask for a recent bank statement or company document if it can't verify you automatically. Have one handy, but most venues aren't asked.
  • Proof of registration — an ASIC company extract or your ABN registration.

The single most common hold-up

Black-and-white scans get rejected. So do photocopies, expired licences, and licence photos where the address or licence number is not readable. Photograph the ID in daylight, flat, all four corners in frame.

Chapter two

Signing in.

Open the link we sent you. You will land on the sign-in screen.

01

Enter your email and password

Use the exact address we set the account up with. Then press Sign in →.

02

You go straight to setting up your venue

The first time you sign in there is nothing on your account yet, so the portal takes you directly to Step 1 of 2 · Set up your venue. That is the next chapter.

Every time after that, signing in takes you to your dashboard.

Forgot your password?

Press Forgot password? next to the password field, enter your sign-in email and press Send reset link →. Open the link in the email — it only works once, so use the newest one if you asked twice — then choose a new password of at least eight characters. You are signed straight in.

To change your password while signed in, use Change password at the bottom of the sidebar. We recommend doing this the first time you sign in, so the password we sent you stops working. The reset email goes to the address on the account, so do not change that address without telling us.

Chapter three

Listing your venue.

This is what guests see when one of your deals surfaces in the app — the photo, the name, the hours, how to find you. It takes about five minutes.

01

Add a hero photo

Click the dashed box marked Drop a photo or click to upload, or drag a file onto it. JPG or PNG, under 5 MB.

This is the single biggest influence on whether someone taps your deal, so use a real photo of the room with people or atmosphere in it rather than a logo. Once it uploads you will see a preview with a Remove button if you want to swap it.

02

Fill in the basics

FieldWhat to put
Venue name *The trading name guests know you by, not the company name. e.g. The Lantern Room
Category *Pick the closest: Bar, Restaurant, Cafe, Bowling, Mini Golf, Escape Room, Arcade, Pool / Billiards, Karaoke, or Other. Defaults to Bar.
Phone optionalA number guests could ring. e.g. (02) 9331 0042
Description optionalOne or two lines. Concrete beats flowery. e.g. Rooftop cocktail bar with harbour views and a late licence.
03

Location and contact

FieldWhat to put
Street address *Must start with the street number. e.g. 112 Crown Street
Suburb *e.g. Surry Hills
Email optionalWhere booking questions should go. e.g. hello@venue.com.au
Website optionalType the full address including https://. e.g. https://myvenue.com.au

If the address is rejected

The form will say "Include a street number, e.g. 123 Main St". Put the number first — write 112 Crown Street, not Crown Street, Surry Hills. Leave the suburb out of this field; it has its own.

The website field needs https://

The hint underneath reads e.g. myvenue.com.au, but typing it that way stops the form with "Please enter a URL." Write https://myvenue.com.au instead, or leave the field empty.

04

Set your opening hours

Seven rows, Monday to Sunday. Each has a switch and two times. Turn the switch off for any day you are closed and the row will read Closed.

Check Sunday. It arrives switched off by default, so if you trade Sundays you have to turn it on. The other days start at 11:00–22:00, with 11:00–23:00 on Friday and 10:00–23:00 on Saturday.

05

Tick any accessibility features

Guests filter on these to find somewhere that suits them, so tick everything that genuinely applies: wheelchair access, step-free entry, accessible bathroom, hearing assistance, vision assistance, a low-sensory or quiet space, and whether you are service-animal friendly.

Only claim what you can deliver — a guest who arrives to find no step-free entry is a worse outcome than not appearing in that filter.

06

Press Create venue

The button sits at the bottom right and reads Create venue →. If anything required is missing, the offending field turns red with a short message underneath.

Once it saves you land on your dashboard, with a banner across the top prompting you to set up payments. That is Step 2 of 2, and it is the next chapter.

You can change this later

Everything on this form can be edited after you press Create venue. Open Venue details in the sidebar, change the name, photo, address, contact details, hours or accessibility features, and press Save changes. Guests see the update straight away.

Chapter four

Getting paid.

Stripe, our payments provider, verifies your business once so bookings can be paid straight into your bank account. It takes about five minutes, and most venues are verified within minutes of finishing.

01

Press Set up payments

Open Payments setup from the sidebar (you land there straight after creating your venue) and press Set up payments.

02

Follow Stripe's steps

Stripe's own form opens inside the page. It asks for your business details and ABN, the people who run or own the business, and the bank account your payouts go to. Stripe checks most of it automatically as you type. If it needs a photo of someone's ID, it asks for it then, and you can take the photo with your phone.

You can stop and come back. Stripe keeps your progress.

03

That's it

When Stripe finishes, the status card turns to Verified and your deals can go live. We email you when it happens, and again if Stripe ever needs anything else.

How the money moves

When a guest books, the full price is held on their card. Nothing is taken yet. When you check them in with their six-digit code, the card is charged and the money is paid out to your bank, usually within minutes. If your bank can't take instant payments, it arrives on the next daily payout instead.

During your free three-month pilot you keep 100% of every booking, and Impulse covers the card and payout fees. After the pilot, Impulse keeps a 10% deposit on each booking and you receive the rest. The payments page shows the date your pilot ends.

While you wait

What the status means.

The status card at the top of the payments page tells you where you stand.

Being verifiedStripe is checking your details, which usually takes a few minutes. Nothing for you to do.
VerifiedYou're set up. Your deals can go live, and check-ins are paid straight to your bank.
Needs attentionStripe needs a little more. The page lists exactly what, and a banner shows the steps to finish.

You can build deals while you wait: write them, price them, save them. They won't go live to guests until you're verified. Nothing is lost by getting your first few deals ready now.

Chapter five

Publishing your first deal.

A deal is a discounted window you would rather fill than leave empty. You choose the price, the hours and how many spots. Nothing goes live that you have not set.

From the dashboard, press + New deal. You can also reach it from Deals in the sidebar.

01

The basics

FieldWhat to put
Title *What the guest is getting, plainly. e.g. Two-for-one cocktails
Category *Same list as your venue category.
Date *Free text, so write it the way a guest would read it. e.g. Tonight · 6–9pm
Description optionalOne line on what is included. e.g. Two signature cocktails for the price of one.
02

Pricing

Enter the original price and the discount percentage, and the form shows you the resulting deal price immediately. The unit defaults to pp — per person — so change it if you are pricing per table or per lane.

Set the discount at what genuinely makes the hour worth filling. Deeper is not always better; you will start to see which windows respond once a few deals have run.

03

Time slots

Type a slot and press Enter to add it. Each becomes a chip you can remove with the ×. e.g. 6:30pm

You need at least one, or the form will not save.

04

Capacity

Total spots is how many you are prepared to give away across the whole deal. Max group size caps how many one booking can take, and defaults to six.

05

Save it

The Deal active switch is on by default. Turn it off to keep the deal as a draft.

Press Save deal. Your deals list shows everything with a toggle to activate or pause, a pencil to edit, and a cross to delete.

Why your first deal shows as inactive

Until Stripe verifies you, the Active toggle stays off no matter what you set on the form. That's the verification gate, not a mistake. The deal is saved and ready, and it goes live once you're verified.

Chapter six

Redeeming a code at the door.

This is the page your floor staff use. Show it to whoever is on the door — it is the only part of the portal they need.

Open Redeem from the sidebar. The guest shows you a six-digit code in their app. Type it into the six boxes; it submits itself as soon as the sixth digit lands. No scanner, no hardware.

What you will see

ResultWhat to do
Valid ticket — enjoy!Let them in. Their card has been charged and the money is on its way to you. If a card couldn't be charged, the screen says how much to collect at the till.
Already redeemedThis code has been used. Check whether a colleague scanned them in already.
Booking cancelledThe guest cancelled. They are not entitled to the deal.
Code not foundRe-type it — most often a digit was mistyped.
Wrong venueThe code belongs to a different venue's deal.

Group bookings

If the code belongs to a group — a huddle — the screen says so before charging anything, and previews every member and the share held on each of their cards.

Check the party in front of you matches, then press Charge group. Each held share is charged at once. Anyone whose card can't be charged is listed clearly so you can take payment from them at the till.

Worth knowing

Checking a guest in is what charges their card and pays you. If a group never checks in, only the 10% deposit is charged, the morning after, and the rest is released back to them. Make sure every guest gets checked in.

Chapter seven

Your dashboard and payouts.

Once you are live, this is the screen you will actually look at.

The four numbers at the top

Bookings todayAcross all your deals.
Revenue todayConfirmed today.
Spots filledHow much of what you offered has gone.
Active dealsWhat is live right now.

Deal performance

Below the recent bookings you will find a card per deal that has run, showing the fill rate and how long it took from going live to the last booking. This is the part worth watching — it tells you which windows and which discounts actually move, so your next deal is a better guess than your last.

Payouts

Money sent to your bank. Each check-in is paid out within minutes, or on the next daily payout if your bank can't take instant payments.

Paid outCleared into your account.
On its waySent, not yet cleared.
Awaiting payoutIn your Stripe balance, not yet sent.

Each payout expands to show the bookings it covers, so you can match it against the guests who came in.
